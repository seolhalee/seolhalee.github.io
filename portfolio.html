Data and tech in public governance

Data is productive of relations and actions in the public sector.

2021–2026PhD dissertation, UC Irvine. Adviser: Dr. Paul Dourish. Book project in progress.

The Hopes and Limits of Data-centric Reform in Public Governance

Data-driven governance is a well-worn promise. My dissertation follows it across public agencies, private firms, and social sector organizations, through three years of ethnographic fieldwork alongside people in these settings. It asks what these actors pursue through the promise and how they carry it out in practice.

The study finds that data-driven governance is pursued as a way to make better decisions under institutional pressure. It also stays vague and fleeting, and it produces new dependencies and relationships. I am currently developing the dissertation into a book.

2022–23Design and ethnographic study

Reconfiguring data relations: Institutional dynamics around data in local governance

Data infrastructures are major sites of city government, but they are embedded in institutional routines that resist abstract critique. This study follows the Neighborhood Data Board, a dashboard I designed and developed with a city planning department in California. It brought data from multiple sources together in a spatial framework to center neighborhood voices that planning often marginalizes. Tracing the negotiations over data’s value revealed tensions within and between groups, and showed how city employees navigated them mainly by adjusting the relations among groups around data, which we call data relations. Narratives in organizational communications were key to revealing and mobilizing those relations. We argue that tracing data relations helps CSCW scholars understand institutional data politics and intervene in systems of power.

2023Conference presentation and paper, Big Data & Society 2025

Efficient service provider or committed social reformer?: Government data storytelling around city data projects

We analyzed how governments present city data initiatives to the public, and treated those presentations as data performances: the means by which governments earn legitimacy to control data’s value and role in cities. The analysis surfaced two distinct narratives that share a storytelling structure. Reading such narratives critically, with attention to the relationship between government and citizens, can serve as a tactic of resistance for communities and activists pursuing data justice.

Place, activism, and digital media

Places are socially constructed through experiences, knowledge, and interactions in physical and digital spaces.

2023Conference paper, CSCW

More than a property: Place-based meaning making and mobilization on social media to resist gentrification

We examined what works, and what struggles, when activists build counter-narratives on social media. Our qualitative analysis of Twitter discourse covered the redevelopment of an old manufacturing district in Seoul, South Korea. The place-based nature of the resistance complicated public mobilization online. Even so, collective meaning-making rooted in people’s own experiences of and relations to the contested place made the cause relevant to people who were neither tenants nor property owners, and it led to political action that affected policy decisions.

Participatory design and planning practice

How can we strengthen the identity and capacity of communities as designers of the places they live?

  • 2020Course project, ICT and Global Development, Georgia Tech

    Digital cultural probing tool: Collecting lived experiences of senior immigrant communities

    Senior immigrants face intersectional barriers to community engagement in urban planning. Eurocentric planning discourages them from reflecting on lived experience in their home countries, and language and cultural gaps get in the way of authentic communication with authorities. We developed a digital cultural probing tool that collects lived experiences, memories, narratives, and artifacts, so that planners can learn about ways of planning outside conventional Western methods.

  • 2018Participatory design workshop, YANGJI.CO

    Returning a gendered playground to all: Participatory design for inclusive playful places

    During an urban regeneration project in Cheonan, South Korea, we ran an open studio where residents could walk in and join the design process alongside architects, planners, and designers. Conversations with elderly women revealed that the neighborhood had no place for them to play. Field investigation with residents showed that most public spaces were highly masculinized: the large shaded area under a bridge served as a gambling yard for older men, and enclosed green spaces went unused by women who felt unsafe there. In the final presentation, we proposed redesigning the public spaces around three principles: make them visually open, connect them into a network accessible to all, and add more inclusive and engaging programs.

  • 2018Mobile app prototype and pilot, YANGJI.CO

    Namsan-Maeul Talk: A mobile app for community engagement

    Namsan-Maeul Talk helps residents understand convoluted urban plans and take part in the process from anywhere. In a series of participatory workshops, many residents told us that plans and announcements were hard to follow, which kept them from discussing the issues with their neighbors. The app gives residents a real-time progress dashboard and proactive notifications about actions or changes to the plans, and it provides a platform for organizing neighborhood events. We piloted it on-site during the workshop period.

  • 2017Participatory workshop and strategic design, YANGJI.CO

    Indigenous eco(-nomic) system: Community mapping and networking of local assets for economic empowerment

    How can a shrinking community on a rural island revive its tourism economy while keeping out an enormous inflow of foreign capital? In a participatory workshop on an island under threat of massive development, we recovered overlooked components of the island’s indigenous ecosystem and built a network of local assets that supports sustainable, profit-making programs. The result inspired a community-led economic development project, which was implemented after the workshop.

  • 2020Interactive map and web development

    COVID-19 Event Risk Planner

    I designed and developed the interactive county-level map for the COVID-19 Event Risk Planner, a web tool built by the Weitz group at Georgia Tech Biosciences. It estimates the risk of gatherings and group events in every US county. The tool drew wide public attention and press coverage.

  • 2021Conference presentation with Andris, C., ACSP Annual Conference

    Estimating diversity at points of interest in Atlanta, Georgia using origin-destination trip data

    Third places such as cafes, bars, parks, and hair salons offer chances to meet diverse people. Which kinds of places, and which neighborhoods, offer more social diversity? Using origin-destination trip data from mobile GPS sensors, we estimated racial and income diversity at third places in Atlanta. Diversity differed by type of place (food outlets versus health care, for example), by residential demographics, and by ongoing neighborhood change. We call for contextualizing these measures by working more closely with community members.

  • 2019Conference paper, UbiComp Adjunct

    Inferring the commercial landscape using age-biased online search results

    What does age-biased place recommendation tell us about a place, and how do different age groups’ restaurant preferences relate to commercial activity? We mined place recommendation results from Naver Place to analyze age-specific restaurant preferences across commercial areas in Seoul. Both high-traffic and rapidly changing areas offer diverse options that appeal to all age groups, and that diversity is driven mainly by the tastes of younger groups.

  • 2020Conference presentation, NARSC

    Who has local, global, glocal sets of connections? Classifying individual spatial and social networks

    Everyone connects to a set of places through travel, migration, the locations of social ties, telecommunication, and more abstract thoughts and proclivities. Collectively, these social flows form a star-like, spatially embedded pattern. We classified the connection sets reported in a survey into distinct typologies and examined how they relate to individual and behavioral factors of social and spatial extensibility. With Liang, X., Andris, C., Chen, H., and de la Peña, B.

  • 2019Course project, Urban Ecological Design

    Augmenting the visual experience of GPS tracks with machine learning

    GPS tracks give granular spatio-temporal information about how people move through cities, but little about what the movement is like. In this project I used machine learning (classification and image segmentation) to infer two kinds of context from GPS tracks: trip purpose and mode, and the visual components encountered along a trip. I processed a day of trips by 860 individuals in Tokyo, Japan, to ask where people pass on their daily trips, what scenes they see, and whether that changes when they commute, run errands, or go out to hang out.
