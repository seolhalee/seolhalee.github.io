Seolha Lee

I’m an interdisciplinary scholar of data, technology, and public governance. Trained in informatics and urban planning, I examine how public institutions develop and use data and technology, and how those technologies in turn reshape governance.

Data-driven governance is a well-worn promise, with real impact on the everyday work of public governance. My dissertation, The Hopes and Limits of Data-centric Reform in Public Governance, follows it across public agencies, private firms, and social sector organizations. Over three years of ethnographic fieldwork, I worked alongside people in these settings to ask what they pursue through this promise and how they carry it out in practice. I found that people turn to it as a way to make better decisions under institutional pressure, and while the goal seems far, it leaves new dependencies and relationships behind. I am now developing the dissertation into a book.

Before my PhD, I practiced as a data analyst in urban planning agencies in South Korea and the US. Currently, I am a Senior Research Associate at the Bloomberg Harvard Center for Cities, where I study data-centric practices and innovation processes in city governance. I completed my PhD in Informatics at UC Irvine in 2026.

Research

  • Data and tech in public governance
    My recent research examines data-centric reform in governments and GovTech partnerships, focusing on the relationships between professional groups and what they mean for public decision-making. The work aims to inform institutional actions for more democratic and effective governance.

My earlier research and practice in urban governance covers:

  • Place, activism, and digital media
    How people make meaning of contested places and mobilize around them online.
  • Participatory design and planning practice
    Workshops, prototypes, and tools built with residents in South Korea and the US.
  • Urban analytics and human experience
    Computational work on social networks, mobility, and the perception of space.
